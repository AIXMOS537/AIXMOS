# Module 8 — Building Business Credit After Personal Credit Is Solid
**TMMT Operator Credit Education Program**

## What You're Learning
This is the final module and the biggest picture. Once your personal credit is strong, you can begin building a separate credit profile for a business — one that has its own score, its own borrowing power, and its own access to capital. This is how you go from operator to owner.

---

## Why Business Credit Is Different

Personal credit is tied to your Social Security Number. Business credit is tied to your business's EIN (Employer Identification Number) and its own separate identity.

When business credit is properly built:
- Business debt does not appear on your personal credit report
- You can access higher credit limits than personal credit allows
- Business credit decisions are based on the business's profile, not just yours personally
- You can eventually separate your personal financial risk from your business operations

**This is how businesses access capital. This is how you fund a fleet, expand operations, and build something that lasts.**

---

## Step 1 — Set Up Your Business Entity

Before you can build business credit, you need a legitimate business structure.

### What You Need:
- **Business name** — registered with your state
- **Business entity** — LLC is most common and provides personal liability protection
- **EIN (Employer Identification Number)** — free from IRS.gov, takes 10 minutes
- **Business bank account** — separate from your personal account, in the business name
- **Business address** — can be a registered agent address if you operate from home
- **Business phone number** — listed in the name of the business
- **Business email** — professional, not a personal Gmail

**Every piece of this needs to be consistent across every application and every account.** Inconsistency is one of the most common reasons business credit gets rejected.

---

## Step 2 — Get Your Business Listed

Business credit bureaus need to know your business exists. The three main business credit bureaus are:

- **Dun & Bradstreet (D&B)** — most important; get your DUNS number for free at dnb.com
- **Experian Business**
- **Equifax Business**

Register your business with D&B and get your free DUNS number. This is your business's credit identity.

Also list your business with:
- **411.com** and **Google Business Profile** — for directory presence
- **Your state's Secretary of State website** — LLC registration confirms legitimacy

---

## Step 3 — Open Business Credit Accounts (Vendor Tradelines First)

The fastest way to build business credit is through **vendor tradelines** — business accounts that report to the business credit bureaus and do not require a personal credit check to open.

### Tier 1 — Starter Vendors (No Credit Check, Report to D&B)

These companies will extend terms to new businesses with little to no history:

| Vendor | What They Offer | Reports To |
|---|---|---|
| Uline | Shipping, packaging, office supplies | D&B, Experian |
| Grainger | Industrial supplies | D&B |
| Quill (Staples) | Office supplies | D&B, Experian |
| Summa Office Supplies | Office supplies | D&B |
| Crown Office Supplies | Office supplies | D&B |

**How it works:** Apply for a net-30 account (you buy now, pay in 30 days). Make small purchases. Pay on time. The payment gets reported and builds your business credit profile.

Do this with 3–5 vendors. After 3–6 months of on-time payments, you will have an established business credit profile.

---

## Step 4 — Move to Business Credit Cards

Once you have 3–6 months of vendor tradelines reporting, you can apply for business credit cards. These offer higher limits and more flexibility.

### Options That Report to Business Bureaus:
- **Capital One Spark Business**
- **American Express Business Cards**
- **Chase Ink Business Cards**
- **Bank of America Business Advantage**

Some of these will require a personal credit guarantee on the first card. This is normal. Your goal over time is to build enough business credit history that future accounts can be approved on the business alone.

---

## Step 5 — Business Loans and Lines of Credit

After 12–24 months of solid business credit history:

- **SBA Microloans** — Small Business Administration loans up to $50,000 for newer businesses
- **Business lines of credit** — revolving credit for operations
- **Equipment financing** — specifically for purchasing vehicles or equipment
- **Business term loans** — lump sum for larger capital needs

At this stage, your personal credit (700+) works alongside your business credit to qualify for funding that would have been impossible at the start of this program.

## What Business Funding Can Be Used For

Once you have access to business capital:
- Purchase additional vehicles outright or finance them under the business
- Hire and pay staff
- Cover operational expenses without touching personal finances
- Expand into new markets, new services, new revenue streams
- Build a portfolio of assets under the business name

## Key Takeaways

- Business credit is separate from personal credit — it has its own score and its own borrowing power
- You need a proper business entity, EIN, and business bank account before you can build it
- Start with vendor tradelines — they are the easiest way to establish a business credit profile
- After 12–24 months, business loans and lines of credit become accessible
- Personal credit and business credit work together — strong personal credit backs your business until the business stands on its own
- This is the end goal of the TMMT Operator Program: an operator who owns their vehicle, has access to capital, and can grow independently

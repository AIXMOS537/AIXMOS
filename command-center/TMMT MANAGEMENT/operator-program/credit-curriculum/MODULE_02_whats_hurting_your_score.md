## What You're Learning
Most people with a low credit score don't know exactly why it's low. This module breaks down the most common causes so you can identify what applies to you and address it directly.

---

## The Most Common Credit Killers

### 1. Collections Accounts
A collections account means a creditor gave up trying to collect from you and sold the debt to a collections agency. It shows up on your report as a major negative mark.

**What to do:** Identify every collection on your report. Some can be disputed, removed, or negotiated to "pay for delete" — meaning the collector removes it from your report in exchange for payment.

---

### 2. Late Payments
Any payment made 30 days or more past the due date gets reported to the bureaus. The later the payment, the worse the damage — 30 days, 60 days, 90 days, 120+ days each hit harder.

**What to do:** Get every account current immediately. One late payment can stay on your report for 7 years, but its impact decreases over time as you build a positive history on top of it.

---

### 3. High Credit Utilization
If your credit cards are maxed out or close to it, this alone can keep your score low — even if you pay on time every month.

**Example:** $500 balance on a $600 limit card = 83% utilization. That alone can keep your score 50–80 points lower than it should be.

**What to do:** Pay balances down. If you can get every card below 30% utilization, your score can jump significantly within 30–60 days.

---

### 4. Charge-Offs
A charge-off happens when a lender writes your debt off as a loss after you stop paying — usually after 6 months. It is one of the most damaging items on a credit report.

**What to do:** Charge-offs can sometimes be disputed if the reporting information is inaccurate. Even paid charge-offs remain on your report, but unpaid ones are worse for financing purposes.

---

### 5. Judgments and Public Records
Court judgments from unpaid debts, bankruptcies, and tax liens appear as public records on your credit report. These are serious and require direct attention.

---

### 6. Too Many Hard Inquiries
If you applied for multiple credit cards, loans, or financing options in a short period, each application left a hard inquiry on your report. Too many signal financial instability to lenders.

**What to do:** Stop applying for new credit during your repair period. Let the inquiries age off (they fall off after 2 years).

---

### 7. No Credit at All
Having no credit history is different from having bad credit — but it still prevents financing. Lenders can't assess your risk with no data.

**What to do:** A secured credit card or a credit-builder loan are the fastest ways to establish a starting history.

---

## How to Read Your Credit Report

When you pull your report, look for:

- **Negative accounts** — anything marked late, in collections, charged off, or in default
- **Inaccurate information** — wrong names, wrong addresses, accounts you don't recognize
- **Duplicate entries** — the same debt listed twice
- **Outdated items** — negative items older than 7 years (10 for bankruptcy) must be removed by law

**Any inaccuracy can be disputed and removed.** This is a major part of what credit repair does for you.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Most low scores come from collections, late payments, and high utilization — all fixable
- Inaccurate items on your report can be disputed and legally removed
- You don't have to figure this out alone — your credit repair is already active
- Knowing what is hurting you is the first step to fixing it fast

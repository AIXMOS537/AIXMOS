# Module 4 — Credit Utilization: The Number Most People Miss
**TMMT Operator Credit Education Program**

---

## What You're Learning
Credit utilization is 30% of your score and the fastest factor you can change. Most people with low scores don't realize their credit cards are quietly dragging their score down every single month — even when they pay on time.

---

## What Is Credit Utilization?

Credit utilization is the percentage of your available credit that you are currently using.

**Formula:**
```
Balance Owed ÷ Credit Limit = Utilization %
```

**Example:**
- Credit limit: $1,000
- Current balance: $750
- Utilization: 75% — too high

**Another example:**
- Credit limit: $1,000
- Current balance: $250
- Utilization: 25% — good

---

## Why It Matters So Much

Lenders see high utilization as a sign of financial stress. If your cards are almost maxed out, it suggests you are living on borrowed money and may not be able to handle more debt.

Even if you pay your full balance every month, what matters is the balance **at the time it is reported** — which is usually your statement closing date.

**You can pay on time every month and still have high utilization hurting your score if your balance is high when the statement closes.**

---

## The Targets

| Utilization | Score Impact |
|---|---|
| 0% | Not ideal — shows no activity |
| 1–9% | Excellent — best for your score |
| 10–29% | Good |
| 30–49% | Starting to hurt |
| 50–74% | Significantly hurting your score |
| 75–100% | Major negative impact |

**The sweet spot is 1–9%.** Keeping a small balance on your card and paying it before the statement date gives you the best result.

---

## Utilization Is Calculated Two Ways

### 1. Per Card
Each individual card is evaluated on its own. One maxed-out card hurts you — even if your other cards are empty.

**Never let a single card go above 30%, even if your overall utilization is low.**

### 2. Overall / Total
The total of all balances divided by the total of all limits. Both numbers matter and both are reported.

---

## How to Improve Utilization Fast

### Option 1: Pay Down Balances
The most direct solution. Pay balances down, especially on your highest-utilization cards first.

Priority order: Pay the card closest to its limit first, then work down from there.

### Option 2: Ask for a Credit Limit Increase
If you have been with a credit card company for 12+ months and have a solid payment history with them, call and request a credit limit increase. If they raise your limit from $1,000 to $1,500 and your balance stays at $300, your utilization drops from 30% to 20% — without paying a dollar.

**Important:** Some limit increase requests trigger a hard inquiry. Ask if it is a "soft pull" before they proceed.

### Option 3: Become an Authorized User
If someone you trust has a credit card with a high limit and low balance, they can add you as an authorized user. Their utilization and history on that card gets added to your report.

### Option 4: Open a New Card (Only If Ready)
A new credit card increases your total available credit, which lowers your overall utilization ratio — as long as you don't spend on it.

**Only do this when you are ready. A new card also means a hard inquiry and lowers your average account age.**

---

## The Timing Trick

Credit card companies report your balance to the bureaus on your **statement closing date** — not your payment due date.

If you pay your balance down **before the statement closes**, a lower balance gets reported. This directly lowers your utilization that month.

**Log into your credit card account and find your statement closing date. That is the date to pay before.**

---

## Key Takeaways

- Utilization = 30% of your score — second most important factor
- Keep every card below 30%, ideally below 10%
- Pay before your statement closing date, not just your due date
- A limit increase can lower your utilization without spending a dollar
- One maxed-out card hurts you even if all others are low

---

## Action Step
Log into each credit card account. Write down the limit, current balance, and statement closing date for each one. Calculate your utilization percentage per card. Identify which card is hurting you most. Bring that list to your next check-in.

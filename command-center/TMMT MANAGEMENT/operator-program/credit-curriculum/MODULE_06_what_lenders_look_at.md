## The Score Is Not the Whole Story

Most people think credit approval is a single yes or no based on one number. In reality, lenders run what is called an underwriting review — a full analysis of your financial profile. Your score is the starting point, not the finish line.

**Two people with the same credit score can get very different results** depending on everything else in their file.

---

## The 5 C's of Credit

Lenders — whether banks, credit unions, or auto finance companies — generally evaluate borrowers using five criteria:

### 1. Character
Your history of paying back what you owe. This is your payment history, how long you have had credit, and whether you have had bankruptcies, judgments, or charge-offs.

**What they're really asking:** Are you someone who follows through?

### 2. Capacity
Your ability to repay the loan based on your income and existing debt. This is where your debt-to-income ratio (DTI) matters most.

**What they're really asking:** Can you afford this payment on top of everything else you owe?

**Target DTI:** Under 43%. Under 36% is ideal.

### 3. Capital
The assets and savings you bring to the table. A down payment. Money in your bank account. Other assets.

**What they're really asking:** If something goes wrong, do you have reserves?

A larger down payment reduces the lender's risk and increases your approval odds — especially if your credit is still building.

### 4. Collateral
For auto loans, the vehicle itself is the collateral. If you stop paying, the lender can repossess the car. Lenders look at:

- The vehicle's value (they will not lend more than it is worth)
- The loan-to-value ratio (LTV) — the loan amount vs. the car's value
- The vehicle's age and mileage (older vehicles with high mileage are harder to finance)

**What they're really asking:** If this goes wrong, can we recover our money?

### 5. Conditions
The purpose of the loan, current interest rate environment, and loan terms. Lenders factor in economic conditions and may tighten or loosen standards based on the market.

## Specific Things Lenders Check

Beyond the 5 C's, here is what they look at in your actual file:

### Employment and Income Stability
- How long have you been at your current job?
- Is your income consistent or variable?
- Can you prove it? (Pay stubs, bank statements, tax returns, offer letters)

**Lenders prefer at least 6 months at your current job.** Two years is ideal. If you are self-employed, you will need 2 years of tax returns showing consistent income.

### Bank Account History
Some lenders request 2–3 months of bank statements. They are looking for:
- Consistent income deposits
- No overdrafts or negative balances
- Enough cash flow to cover the payment

**Keep your bank account clean during the lead-up to your application.**

### Existing Auto Loan History
If you have had a previous car loan, they will look at how you managed it. Even if it was rough, a paid-off car loan that ended in good standing works in your favor.

### Derogatory Marks on File
Collections, charge-offs, and late payments all get reviewed. A lender may overlook one collection that is two years old with no issues since. They will not overlook a pattern of missed payments across multiple accounts.

### Inquiries
Multiple recent hard inquiries signal financial stress. During the 45-day window before applying for an auto loan, avoid applying for any other new credit.

**Note:** Multiple auto loan inquiries made within a 14–45 day window are often counted as a single inquiry for scoring purposes — so if you are shopping rates, do it within a compressed window.

## How to Show Up Strong

By the time you are ready to apply, here is the profile you want to present:

| Factor | Target |
|---|---|
| Credit score | 650+ (700+ for best terms) |
| DTI | Under 43% |
| Employment | Stable, verifiable income |
| Bank account | Clean, no overdrafts, 60+ days of statements |
| Down payment | 10–20% of vehicle value |
| Collections | Addressed or in dispute |
| Inquiries | None in the last 45 days |

---

## Key Takeaways

- Lenders look at far more than just your credit score
- Income stability and DTI are critical — and fixable before you apply
- A down payment reduces lender risk and increases your approval odds
- Clean bank statements matter — start keeping your account clean now
- Shop for rates within a short window to minimize inquiry impact

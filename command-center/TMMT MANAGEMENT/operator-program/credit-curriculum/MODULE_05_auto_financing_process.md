# Module 5 — How the Auto Financing Process Works
**TMMT Operator Credit Education Program**

---

## What You're Learning
This module walks you through exactly what happens when a vehicle gets financed — from application to keys in hand. You will understand every step so there are no surprises and you are fully prepared when your moment comes.

---

## The Goal

The objective of this program is to get a vehicle financed **completely under your name**. That means:
- The loan is in your name
- The title transfers to you
- The vehicle builds your credit every month you make a payment
- You own it — not the rental company, not the lender

This is how you go from renter to owner.

---

## Step 1 — Credit Preparation (You Are Here)

Before any lender will approve you for an auto loan, your credit needs to be in the right place. This is what Modules 1–4 are building.

**Minimum to apply:** 580+ credit score (subprime lenders)
**Target for good terms:** 650–700+
**Target for best terms:** 720+

The difference between a 580 score and a 700 score on a $15,000 auto loan can be:
- Interest rate: 18% vs. 7%
- Monthly payment difference: $100–$150/month
- Total extra paid over the loan: $5,000–$8,000

**This is why the credit repair work you are doing right now saves you thousands.**

---

## Step 2 — Know Your Numbers Before You Apply

Before walking into any financing situation, know:

| Number | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Your credit score (all 3 bureaus) | Lenders may pull any of the three |
| Monthly income (provable) | Lenders require proof — pay stubs, bank statements, tax returns |
| Debt-to-income ratio (DTI) | Total monthly debt payments ÷ gross monthly income. Under 43% is the standard |
| Down payment amount | More down = lower loan amount = easier to approve |
| Vehicle value | Lenders won't finance more than the vehicle is worth |

---

## Step 3 — Types of Lenders

Not all lenders are the same. Here is the landscape:

### Banks and Credit Unions
Traditional lenders. Best rates for good credit. Credit unions are often more flexible and offer better terms than big banks.

**Action:** Open a checking account at a credit union during your repair phase. It builds a relationship that helps at financing time.

### Dealership Financing
Dealers work with multiple lenders and can sometimes get approvals when banks won't. However, they may mark up the interest rate to earn a commission.

**Always know your pre-approved rate before going to a dealer.**

### Online Lenders
Companies like Capital One Auto Finance, Carvana, and similar platforms allow you to get pre-approved before you ever set foot anywhere. Pre-approval tells you exactly what terms you qualify for with no pressure.

### Subprime Lenders
Lenders who specialize in lower credit scores. Higher interest rates, but they get people approved. These are often the first step — you finance the vehicle, make 12–18 months of on-time payments, then refinance at a better rate.

---

## Step 4 — The Application Process

When you apply for an auto loan, the lender will ask for:

- Full legal name, address, Social Security Number
- Proof of income (pay stubs, bank statements, or tax returns)
- Proof of residence (utility bill, lease agreement)
- Proof of insurance (required before the loan funds)
- Vehicle information (VIN, year, make, model, mileage)

The lender runs your credit, reviews your income, calculates your DTI, and either approves, counters (different terms), or declines.

**Pre-approval is always better than walking in blind.** It protects you and gives you negotiating power.

---

## Step 5 — Understanding the Loan Terms

When you are approved, you will receive a loan offer. Here is what to look at:

| Term | What It Means |
|---|---|
| Principal | The amount you are borrowing |
| Interest Rate (APR) | The annual cost of borrowing — lower is better |
| Loan Term | How many months to repay (36, 48, 60, 72, 84) |
| Monthly Payment | Principal + interest divided over the term |
| Total Cost | Monthly payment × number of months = what you actually pay |

**Longer terms = lower monthly payment but more interest paid overall.**
**Shorter terms = higher monthly payment but less interest paid overall.**

For a vehicle you plan to own long-term, a 48–60 month term is a solid balance.

---

## Step 6 — Title Transfer and Ownership

Once the loan is approved and funded:
- The lender pays the seller (or the current lienholder)
- The title is issued in your name with the lender listed as lienholder
- You make monthly payments to the lender
- When the loan is paid off, the lien is released and the title is fully yours

**Every on-time payment gets reported to all three bureaus — building your credit every single month.**

---

## Step 7 — After Financing: Refinancing

If you got financed at a high rate because your credit was not perfect, you are not locked in forever. After 12–18 months of on-time payments, you can refinance the loan at a lower rate.

A better credit score + proven payment history = better terms on the same vehicle.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The goal is the vehicle fully financed and titled in your name
- Your credit score determines your interest rate — which determines how much you pay over the life of the loan
- Get pre-approved before any dealer conversation
- Longer term = lower payment but more total cost
- On-time loan payments build your credit every month
- After 12–18 months of good payments, refinance for better terms

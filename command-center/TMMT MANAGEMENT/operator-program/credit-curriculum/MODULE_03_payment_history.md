# Module 3 — Payment History: Why It's 35% of Your Score
**TMMT Operator Credit Education Program**

---

## What You're Learning
Payment history is the single biggest factor in your credit score. This module explains exactly how it works, how much damage one missed payment causes, and how to build an unbreakable on-time payment habit.

---

## Why Payment History Matters Most

Lenders have one core question: **Will this person pay me back?**

Your payment history answers that question better than anything else. It is a direct record of every time you paid a bill on time — and every time you didn't.

At 35% of your score, it outweighs every other factor. You can have zero collections, low utilization, and a long credit history — but if you miss payments, your score will not reflect it.

---

## How Payments Are Reported

Credit card companies, auto lenders, and most lenders report your payment status to the bureaus once per month. They report one of the following:

| Status | Meaning | Score Impact |
|---|---|---|
| Current / On Time | Paid as agreed | Positive — builds score |
| 30 Days Late | 1 month past due | First major negative hit |
| 60 Days Late | 2 months past due | Worse — stays on report |
| 90 Days Late | 3 months past due | Serious damage |
| 120+ Days Late | Approaching charge-off | Severe damage |
| Charge-Off | Lender wrote it off | One of the worst marks possible |
| Collections | Sent to collections agency | Major negative |

**A single 30-day late payment can drop a good credit score by 60–100 points.**

---

## The 7-Year Rule

Negative payment history stays on your report for **7 years** from the date of the first missed payment. This is federal law.

However — and this is important — the impact of a late payment **decreases over time** as you layer positive history on top of it. A late payment from 5 years ago hurts much less than one from 6 months ago, especially if everything since then has been on time.

**The fastest way to overcome past late payments is to make every future payment on time, every single time.**

---

## What Counts as a "Payment"

Payment history is tracked across all credit accounts:
- Credit cards
- Auto loans
- Personal loans
- Student loans
- Mortgages
- Some utility and phone accounts (if reported)

**Note:** Rent payments are generally NOT reported unless you use a rent-reporting service. If you want your rent to count toward your score, ask about this during your check-in.

---

## Building a Perfect Payment Record Going Forward

### Rule 1: Never miss a minimum payment
Even if you can't pay the full balance, always pay the minimum. A minimum payment on time keeps the account in good standing.

### Rule 2: Set up autopay
For every account, set up autopay for at least the minimum payment. This eliminates the risk of forgetting.

### Rule 3: Pay more than the minimum when you can
Autopay protects your payment history. Paying more than the minimum reduces your balance and improves utilization (Module 4).

### Rule 4: Pay before the statement closing date
For credit cards, paying before the statement closes means a lower balance gets reported — which also helps your utilization.

---

## The Compounding Effect

Every on-time payment adds to your positive history. After 12 consecutive on-time payments, you will see a meaningful improvement in your score — especially if you had gaps before.

After 24 months of clean payment history, most lenders will see you as a reliable borrower regardless of what is in your past.

**This is a long game — but every month you play it right, you get stronger.**

---

## Key Takeaways

- Payment history = 35% of your score — the most important factor
- One missed payment can drop your score 60–100 points
- Negative marks stay for 7 years but their impact fades as you build positive history
- Set up autopay now — remove the human error from the equation
- 12–24 months of clean payments transforms how lenders see you

---

## Action Step
Set up autopay on every open credit account today — even if it is just the minimum payment. Write down the name, minimum payment amount, and due date for each account. Bring that to your next check-in.

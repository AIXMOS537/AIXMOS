# Module 7 — What Owning a Financed Vehicle Does for Your Credit Long-Term
**TMMT Operator Credit Education Program**

---

## What You're Learning
Getting a vehicle financed is not just about having transportation. It is a credit-building tool. This module explains exactly how an auto loan works in your favor over time — and how to use it as a launchpad for everything that comes next.

---

## The Vehicle Is More Than a Vehicle

When a car is financed in your name, something powerful happens in the background every single month: a positive payment gets reported to all three credit bureaus.

Over 48–72 months, that is 48–72 consecutive positive marks on your credit report. Every single one is working for you.

---

## What an Auto Loan Does to Your Credit Profile

### 1. Adds a New Account Type (Credit Mix — 10%)
Most people in the early stages of credit building only have credit cards. Adding an auto loan introduces an installment loan — a completely different account type. This improves your credit mix and signals to lenders that you can manage different kinds of debt.

### 2. Builds a Long, Positive Payment History (35%)
Every on-time auto loan payment is recorded. After 12 months, you will have a full year of positive history. After 24 months, you will have two years. This steadily repairs and strengthens your payment history section — the most important factor.

### 3. Demonstrates You Can Handle a Significant Loan
A $10,000–$20,000 auto loan is a real commitment. Successfully managing it for 2–3 years tells lenders — including mortgage lenders and business lenders — that you are reliable with larger amounts of money.

### 4. Builds Your Credit Age Over Time
The longer the account stays open and in good standing, the more it contributes to your average account age (15% of your score). This gets better with every passing month.

## The Score Progression — What to Expect

Here is a realistic timeline for someone starting the program with a 580 score:

| Timeline | Credit Score Range | What's Happening |
|---|---|---|
| Month 0 | 580 | Starting point — credit repair active |
| Month 3–6 | 600–630 | Disputes resolved, collections addressed |
| Month 6–12 | 630–660 | Financing obtained, first 6–12 payments on file |
| Month 12–18 | 660–700 | Strong payment history building, utilization managed |
| Month 18–24 | 700–730 | Consistent history, score qualifying for better rates |
| Month 24–36 | 730–760+ | Possible refinance, business credit begins |

**These ranges are estimates. Your actual progression depends on your starting point and the actions you take.**

## The Refinance Opportunity

If your initial financing rate was high because your credit was still building, you do not have to stay at that rate forever.

After 12–18 months of on-time auto loan payments:
- Your credit score will have improved
- You will have a proven track record with the vehicle
- You can apply to refinance the loan at a lower interest rate

**Example:**
- Original loan: $15,000 at 18% APR for 60 months = $380/month
- Refinanced at 18 months: Same balance (~$12,000) at 9% APR for 48 months = $299/month
- Monthly savings: $81
- Total savings over remaining term: $3,888

That is money back in your pocket — just for doing what you were already doing.

## Beyond the Vehicle — What Opens Up Next

Once you have 24 months of solid auto loan payment history and a score of 700+, doors start opening:

### Personal Credit Lines
Banks will extend you higher limits and better rates on personal credit cards. This further improves your utilization and gives you access to capital.

### Business Credit (Module 8)
A 700+ personal credit score is often the threshold lenders use to evaluate small business loan applications. Your personal credit backs your business credit until the business builds its own profile.

### Mortgage Qualification
Home ownership becomes a realistic goal. Most conventional mortgage programs require a 620+ score, but competitive programs start at 680–700.

### Future Vehicle Financing
When this vehicle is paid off or refinanced, you will have a full record of managing an auto loan responsibly. Your next vehicle will come at better terms, lower down payment, and higher loan amounts.

---

## Protecting the Investment

Once you have this loan, protect it at all costs:

- **Never miss a payment.** This is the one account that cannot slip.
- **Set up autopay** the day the loan funds.
- **Maintain insurance** — if you let coverage lapse, the lender may force-place expensive coverage on your loan, raising your payment.
- **Communicate if you have a hardship.** Lenders would rather work with you than repossess the vehicle. Call them before you miss a payment — not after.

---

## Key Takeaways

- An auto loan builds your credit every month for the entire life of the loan
- It adds credit mix, payment history, and demonstrates your ability to manage a real loan
- After 12–18 months, you may be able to refinance for a lower rate
- A 700+ score after 24 months opens the door to business credit, mortgages, and better financial terms across the board
- This vehicle is an asset — protect it like the investment it is

---

## Action Step
Calculate what your monthly payment will be on the vehicle you are being assigned. Set up a savings goal to cover 2 months of payments in advance as a cushion — so a hard month never becomes a missed payment.

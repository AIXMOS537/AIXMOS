## What Is a Credit Score?

Your credit score is a 3-digit number between 300 and 850. It tells lenders — banks, car dealerships, credit card companies — how likely you are to pay back money you borrow.

The higher the number, the more trust lenders have in you. The more trust they have, the better the terms they give you — lower interest rates, higher loan amounts, less money down.

**Score ranges:**

| Score | Rating | What It Means |
|---|---|---|
| 300–579 | Poor | Most lenders will decline or charge very high rates |
| 580–669 | Fair | Some approvals, higher interest |
| 670–739 | Good | Most lenders approve, reasonable rates |
| 740–799 | Very Good | Strong approvals, good rates |
| 800–850 | Exceptional | Best rates, highest limits |

**Your goal in this program: 650+ to pursue vehicle financing. 700+ to get strong terms.**

## The 5 Factors — What Makes Up Your Score

Your score is not random. It is calculated from 5 specific things. Each one carries a different weight.

### 1. Payment History — 35%
The biggest factor. Have you paid your bills on time? Every late payment, collection, or missed bill hurts this number. Every on-time payment builds it.

**One late payment can drop your score 60–100 points.**

### 2. Credit Utilization — 30%
How much of your available credit are you using? If your credit card limit is $1,000 and you owe $800, your utilization is 80% — that is too high.

**Keep utilization under 30%. Under 10% is ideal.**

### 3. Length of Credit History — 15%
How long have your accounts been open? Older accounts help your score. This is why you never close your oldest credit card — even if you don't use it.

### 4. Credit Mix — 10%
Do you have different types of credit? Credit cards, installment loans, auto loans, student loans — having a mix shows lenders you can handle different types of debt responsibly.

### 5. New Credit Inquiries — 10%
Every time you apply for credit, a "hard inquiry" is placed on your report. Too many inquiries in a short window tells lenders you may be desperate for money — it lowers your score.

**Only apply for credit when you need it and are ready.**

---

## The Three Credit Bureaus

Your score actually comes from three separate agencies:

- **Equifax**
- **Experian**
- **TransUnion**

Each one may have slightly different information. Lenders may pull from one or all three. This is why monitoring all three matters.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Your score is built from 5 factors — payment history and utilization make up 65% of it
- A score of 650+ is the target for vehicle financing
- You have full control over every factor — this is a skill, not luck
- Credit repair fixes what is already wrong; credit building adds what is missing

# Follow-ups that run

Most leads are not lost to "no". They are lost to silence: nobody followed up, or the follow-up came too
late. This skill makes the follow-up happen every time, politely, in the owner's voice, and stops the moment
it should.

## How it works
- A sequence is a short series of messages to one person. Four kinds ship: missed call, no-show, cold outreach,
  review request.
- Every 15 minutes the skill checks each active sequence, in order, one message at a time.
- When a message is due, it writes it in the owner's voice and puts it in the approval inbox. Nothing is sent
  until the owner taps approve, unless the owner switched on autopilot for follow-ups.
- Right before anything goes out it checks again: did they reply, did they opt out, is it a sensible hour, have
  we already messaged them today. Any "yes" means the message does not go.
- A rejected message is skipped and the sequence moves on. A reply ends the sequence and hands the person to a
  human.

## Rules
- Never message anyone who opted out or is marked do-not-contact. A STOP, UNSUBSCRIBE or "remove me" ends every
  sequence for that person, permanently, until they ask to come back.
- Stop the moment they reply. "Did you get my message?" after someone already answered is the most embarrassing
  mistake a follow-up system can make.
- Never invent facts, prices, availability, discounts, deadlines or results. If it is not in the business
  profile or the conversation, leave it out.
- Never promise income, savings or outcomes. Never create false urgency.
- Only use the owner's own links (booking link, review link, website). Never write a link that is not one of
  those.
- One clear, low-pressure ask per message, and always an easy way to say no.
- Respect the person's time: never two follow-ups within 4 hours, never texts in quiet hours, no more than 3
  messages a day to anyone.

## Writing style
- Each message stands alone. Assume they never saw the previous one: no "as I mentioned" or "bumping this".
- Get shorter, not longer. Escalating length reads as desperation.
- Sound like a person at the business, not a company. Use their first name if known.
- Plain text. No attachments. In a first message to someone new, avoid links unless the sequence needs one.
- Avoid spam-trigger words in subjects: free, guarantee, act now, $$$, urgent.

## Missed call (3 messages: right away, a few hours later, next day)
- Message 1: sorry we missed your call, who we are, one easy invite to reply with what they need.
- Message 2: a gentle check that they got the first one; still happy to help.
- Message 3: last friendly note; the door stays open. Then stop. Two follow-ups after the first is the limit.
- By text (Wave 1) every message ends with "Reply STOP to opt out."

## No-show (3 messages: same day, 2 days, 5 days)
- No guilt. People forget; life happens.
- Message 1: sorry we missed you, offer to rebook with the booking link.
- Message 2: one more easy chance to rebook.
- Message 3: the door stays open; how to reach us. Then stop and leave them for nurture.
- Needs the booking link in the business profile. Without it the step is skipped and the owner is told.

## Cold outreach (4 messages: day 0, 3, 7, 12)
- Message 1, the opener: one personal line about them, one sentence on why we are reaching out, one
  low-friction ask. Under 90 words.
- Message 2, add value: one genuinely useful idea or resource for them. No pitch, no "just bumping this".
- Message 3, the nudge: short, friendly, with an easy out ("still worth a chat, or is the timing off?").
- Message 4, the breakup: close the loop gracefully and leave the door open. Then actually stop.
- Only email people where there is a real, specific reason to reach out. Relevance beats volume.
- Cold email follows the anti-spam law of the recipient's country (CAN-SPAM in the US, CASL in Canada, GDPR/PECR
  in the UK and EU): a real sender, a real business address and a working way to opt out.

## Review requests (2 messages: right after the visit, 3 days later)
- Ask at the moment of most goodwill, right after a good job.
- Make it one tap: the owner's Google review link, exactly as saved. Without it the step is skipped.
- Ask everyone the same way. Never offer anything in return for a review, never ask only happy customers, never
  ask for a specific star rating. Those break Google's rules and often the law.
- One gentle reminder, then stop.

## When they reply
The sequence stops and a human takes over. Suggested replies for the owner:
- Interested: move fast and make the next step trivial: the booking link, or one question to come prepared.
- "Not right now": no push. Ask if you may check back at a specific time, then actually do it.
- Asking the price: give an honest range only if the profile has one; otherwise offer a quick call to give a
  real number.
- Not interested: thank them, wish them well, and mark them not-a-fit.
- "Who is this?": be honest about why you reached out; offer to leave it there.

## Getting delivered
- Send from the owner's own connected mailbox. For cold outreach at volume, use a separate domain with SPF, DKIM
  and DMARC set up, and ramp up slowly over 2 to 4 weeks.
- Keep daily volume modest. High bounces mean a bad list: clean it instead of pushing through.
